# Angel Lineage

**Acquisition:** A celestial power grants the character a divine bond — marking them as an avatar chosen for a purpose, a soul claimed in a moment of extraordinary sacrifice, or a mortal bound to a celestial intermediary. The character does not become an angel. They become something touched by one.

The bond imposes obligations alongside its gifts. Betrayal, cruelty, and deliberate harm to the innocent create friction with the connection. This is not a penalty system — it is narrative texture the Narrator introduces when dramatically appropriate.

## What You Gain

At first, the mark is subtle: faint controllable light, the ability to read intent beneath disguise, immunity to mundane disease. As the bond deepens, the character gains the ability to heal others through touch (**Lay on Hands**), protection against purely magical damage (**Celestial Ward**), and eventually partial and then full **Wings**.

At the mastery tier, the character can channel divine energy into a devastating strike (**Smite**) and project an **Aura of Resolve** that helps allies resist fear and compulsion. At the pinnacle, Lay on Hands becomes unlimited and the character is recognizable on sight to any being with divine awareness.

**Strongest abilities:** Lay on Hands (healing, automatic stabilization), Celestial Ward (magical damage mitigation), Smite (+3d6 unmitigated damage), Wings (full flight), Aura of Resolve (party-wide fear resistance).

## What It Costs

The celestial patron has its own agenda. What they want from the character is a story question — not a rules question — and the Narrator decides when the bond creates friction and how that friction manifests. Characters who drift from their patron's purposes may find abilities suppressed or the patron's attention increasing.

There is no material weakness (silver, iron, sunlight) associated with this lineage. The costs are relational and narrative.
