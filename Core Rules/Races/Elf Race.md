# Elf Race

## Origin

Luniel made the Elves, and she made them in the truest expression of her nature — not the pastoral, gentle notion of life that mortals tend to imagine when they think of her, but life as she actually is: the thing that persists, that endures, that refuses ordinary limits. She is also the maker of Vampires and Werewolves, and there is a pattern in her work if you know to look for it. Luniel does not make things that end easily.

She poured that same quality into a place as much as a people. Three kingdoms — Ard Eliendel, Ard Ilien, and Ard Turiel — built inward around a shared sea, facing each other across water the Elves named for the sky they have spent uncounted centuries studying. The Sea of Stars is the center of everything Elvish: commerce, ceremony, culture, the refined arguments of philosophers who have had a thousand years to sharpen their positions. The Three Kingdoms are not simply where Elves happen to live. They are where Luniel's blessing lives, and the Elves live inside it.

This distinction matters more than it first appears. Elvish immortality is not a property of Elvish bodies. It is a property of the ground beneath their feet, the water they sail, the stars that name their sea. Leave the Three Kingdoms — truly leave, not wander to the border and return — and what follows is quiet and inevitable. Luniel's blessing does not travel. Without it, the body discovers what it would have been all along.

The Elves have no word for this phenomenon, because it has never happened in sufficient numbers to name. Those who leave are called Wanderers. Every one of them walked away alone. None followed in enough numbers to require a term. Their language has no word for diaspora.

They stand close to seven feet tall, lean and tightly muscled — built less like soldiers and more like something that has been refining itself for centuries and arrived at efficiency. There is nothing ethereal about them up close. When an Elf walks into a room, people notice, and then they take a small, involuntary step back, and then they wonder why they did that. The answer is simple: seven feet of patient, unhurried muscle with eyes that have watched empires rise and fall has a way of recalibrating a room's sense of what is dangerous. Elves are rarely threatening. They don't need to be.

They are also not what they were.

Fifteen hundred years ago, something fell from the sky above Anar — not a meteor, though it moved like one. It was a Rift Stone: a fragment of the boundary that separates Anar from whatever lies beyond it, carrying creation magic from both sides of that boundary, Anaric and Demonic in equal measure, wholly corrupt. A mage found it. What he did with it required the combined armies of the Elves, the Dwarves, and the Humans to undo.

When it was over, the stone lay in three pieces.

The Dwarves did what Dwarves do: they dropped their shard into the deepest mine shaft they could find and filled it with stone. The Humans carried theirs across the sea toward Katakhor, intending to leave it somewhere it could do no further harm. Their ship was lost in a storm. The shard went to the bottom of a sea that has no bottom.

The Elves took theirs home to Ard Ilien. They placed it on a pedestal in a clearing, under guard, and began to study it. They were the oldest people on Anar. They had the patience for careful work and the memory to record everything they learned. They assumed they were safe. That their knowledge, wisdom, and immortality would protect them. They were wrong.

The corruption seeped out slowly — slowly enough that no one recognized what was happening until it had already happened. It spread from Ard Ilien across the Sea of Stars, through Ard Eliendel, through Ard Turiel, into every Elf alive. It severed their connection to the Magesea — not completely, not cleanly, but enough. What had once flowed freely through them now flickered like a candle in the wrong kind of wind. What remains is called Arcane Echo: the memory of something that no longer fully answers when called.

The physical cost came with it. The fragility that Luniel had never built into her children began to surface in ways their constitution could not compensate for.

The worst of it was the silence. There has not been a child born to the Elves in fifteen hundred years. The corruption did not kill them. It simply ensured that when the last of them dies, there will be no one to follow.

And then it did something more insidious still. It made them afraid. Fear is not a thing Luniel built into her children — they watched empires fall without flinching. But the corruption moved into the deep places of Elvish psychology and set up residence there, and what emerged looked less like the people Luniel made and more like a civilization pulling its doors closed. The Three Kingdoms — already inward-facing, already built around a shared center — turned inward further. Outsiders were regarded with suspicion. Counsel was distrusted. The very advisors who might have helped were kept at a distance that felt, from the inside, like wisdom.

The stone is still there. It sits on its pedestal in Ard Ilien, still under guard, still being studied. The Elves have spent fifteen hundred years looking for an answer in it. The corruption it produces has made them too fractured, too fearful, and too suspicious of one another to agree on any other course. They cannot move it. They cannot destroy it. They cannot ask for help without the paranoia whispering that help is a vector for something worse.

This is what the Elves carry. Not just the grief of perfect memory and accumulated loss — though that is real, and heavy. They carry the knowledge that they are the last generation, and that the reason they cannot act is the same reason they cannot act.

The Wanderers left knowing all of this. Whether that makes leaving an act of despair or of hope is a question each of them answers differently.

**Size:** Medium
**Base Movement Speed:** 35 ft
**Languages:** Common, Elvish

## Traits

### Arcane Echo
What the Magesea once was for the Elves, it is no longer — but something remains. Spellcasting I is unlocked at no cost. +2 on Supernatural Detection Opposition Checks when attempting to detect the presence and type of magical creatures.

### Immortal Vitality
Luniel built her children to endure. Elves within the Three Kingdoms gain +20 Maximum Health. See [The Price of Leaving](#the-price-of-leaving).

### Intimidating Presence
Seven feet of patient, unhurried muscle produces an effect that has nothing to do with intent. +4 on Social Opposition Checks where size and physical bearing are relevant.

### Living Memory
Elven memory never fades. A conversation from eight hundred years ago is as clear as one from this morning. +2 on Research Opposition Checks.

### Longstrider
Seven feet of leg and arm produces a natural advantage in both movement and reach. Base movement speed is 35 ft instead of 30 ft. Melee weapon reach is increased by an additional 5 ft.

## Racial Flaws

### Conspicuous
Seven feet tall is not a height that disappears into a crowd. −2 on Stealth Opposition Checks. Elves cannot benefit from cover provided by standard humanoid obstacles.

### Distant
The same imperious bearing that makes Elves formidable in confrontation makes them nearly impossible to approach with genuine warmth. The corruption deepened this — what began as natural dignity has, in many Elves, curdled into suspicion. −2 on Persuasion and Negotiation Opposition Checks.

### Faded
The Rift Stone's corruption severed the Elves' connection to the Magesea, and the physical cost followed. Elves are fragile in ways their constitution cannot compensate for — a fragility Luniel never intended and cannot reach across the distance to repair. −4 on Fortitude Opposition Checks.

### Grief
Immortality and perfect memory are a heavy combination. Every loss an Elf has ever suffered remains as vivid as the day it happened — and fifteen hundred years of silence where children's laughter used to be is a loss that never stops arriving. −2 on Willpower Opposition Checks against mind-affecting abilities.

### The Price of Leaving *(mandatory)*
An Elf who departs Ard Eliendel, Ard Ilien, or Ard Turiel permanently surrenders their immortality. Among the Elves, such individuals are known as Wanderers — their language has no word for diaspora. Immortal Vitality provides no benefit outside the Three Kingdoms. As long as the stone remains in Ard Ilien, a Wanderer cannot return without the corruption reclaiming what they have recovered. This flaw is not selected — it applies automatically to every Wanderer.
