# The Fae

*Creature — Living Light*

*"She looked like a woman made of stars. When she moved, a thousand lights shifted with her, the way breath moves across still water. She sat across from me, poured tea she shouldn't have been able to pour, and asked me things I didn't know I'd forgotten."*  
*— Aldric Wenne, Wanderer of Ard Eliendel*

*"She is not a goddess. She is not mortal. She is something the world made for itself, and she has been here longer than most things that claim to be eternal."*  
*— attributed to the White Stag*

## Attacks

**Attack pool:** 1d10 + 5 (Hard Light — melee or ranged, 60 ft.)  
**Defender options:** Dodge or Block  
**On hit:** Hard Light strikes deal 4d8 damage. Hard Light bypasses Natural Armor and is unaffected by magical resistance or damage reduction of any kind.

The Fae does not initiate combat. She defends herself, those in her care, and allies of the White Stag. She deploys Hard Light with economy and precision — one strike is typically sufficient to communicate that further aggression is inadvisable.

## Description

The Fae is not a single being so much as a collective will. She is thousands of Sydh — the tiny pinpricks of living light that migrated to Anar with the Kith and Kin — coalescing into the form of an ethereal woman. She is tall and luminous, her outline softly radiant, her features clear and kind. When she is still, the lights that comprise her drift in slow patterns, like embers rising from a fire that will never go out. When she moves, they shift with her in one fluid motion, a single intention expressed in thousands of points of light.

She pours tea. She sits. She listens. For most mortals, the dissonance between what they are looking at and how it behaves is the most unsettling thing about her. She makes no attempt to resolve it.

## Behavior

The Fae does not seek encounters with mortals — but she does not avoid them either. She appears where she wishes to appear, and those she chooses to speak with are, by her reckoning, being given something worth having. She asks questions more than she answers them. She never lies. She will not be manipulated, coerced, or deceived, and she responds to attempts at any of the three with patient amusement rather than offense.

She is a close friend and ally of the White Stag and serves his cause by her own choice. Those who carry the White Stag's trust are received by The Fae as guests rather than strangers.

She does not age. She does not tire. She has watched the stars move for longer than the eldest Elf can remember, and nothing about the passage of time appears to trouble her.

## Notes

1. Physical attacks, magical attacks, and damage of all types have no effect on The Fae. She cannot be harmed by conventional means.
2. The Fae automatically succeeds on all Willpower Opposition Checks. Her Willpower score of 10 is listed for reference; no Opposition Check will exceed it.
3. Hard Light constructs can take any form The Fae chooses. As barriers, they provide full cover for any creature she designates. As restraints, the target must win a Strength Opposition Check contested against The Fae's Strength to break free.
4. Despite her incorporeal nature, The Fae can touch, lift, carry, and manipulate objects as a mortal would.
5. The Faeries of Anar are The Fae's descendants. This is not common knowledge.
6. The Fae has no connection to Ellessien. Narrators should not assume one.
