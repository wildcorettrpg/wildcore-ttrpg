# Personal Character Flaws

Personal character flaws are chosen at character creation and are distinct from racial flaws. They represent the specific habits, fears, compulsions, and character failings that define who this person is — not what their blood dictates.

A character must select at least one personal flaw at creation. For each additional flaw selected beyond the first, the character gains one bonus Skill Point to spend at character creation. This bonus is capped at three additional flaws — a maximum of three bonus Skill Points, for a character who enters play with four personal flaws.

A character may voluntarily take more than four personal flaws, but receives no further Skill Points for doing so.

Personal flaws are not removed through skill point investment. They are removed only through narrative milestones — specific events in the character's story that confront the flaw directly and give the character cause to change. The Narrator determines when a milestone has been reached, in collaboration with the player. A flaw that has never been confronted cannot be removed. When a flaw is removed this way, the character gains one Skill Point.

Full rules for personal flaws appear in [Character Creation](../Characters/Character%20Creation.md).

## Combat Behaviors

### Reckless
This character doesn't back down. Ever. It has cost them before and it will cost them again, and they know it, and it hasn't changed anything.

−2 on Opposition Checks when attempting to disengage, retreat, or avoid a direct physical confrontation.

### Cowardly
When things go badly, this character's first instinct is survival — their own. They are not proud of this. It doesn't stop them.

−2 on Willpower Opposition Checks to hold a position under fire, stand their ground when outnumbered, or remain in a fight that appears to be lost.

### Bloodthirsty
This character doesn't just want to win. They want to see it finished. Once per combat, when an enemy is downed but not dead, the character must succeed on a Willpower Opposition Check against difficulty 15 or spend their next action ensuring the kill. During that action, −2 on all other Opposition Checks.

### Soft-Hearted
This character cannot bring themselves to hurt someone who hasn't hurt them first. Once the violence starts they manage — but initiating it, or finishing it when the other side is clearly beaten, costs them something they notice.

−2 on attack Opposition Checks against humanoids who have not yet attacked the character this combat. Must succeed on a Willpower Opposition Check against difficulty 15 to deal a killing blow to a humanoid enemy.

### Overprotective
When someone this character cares about is in danger, rational thought exits the situation. They will put themselves between a threat and an ally before they have consciously decided to do so.

When an ally is directly threatened, the character must succeed on a Willpower Opposition Check against difficulty 15 or immediately act to protect them — regardless of whether doing so is tactically sound. −2 on attack Opposition Checks when an ally is in the line of fire.

## Social Behaviors

### Rabble-rouser
Wherever this character goes, things get louder and more chaotic than the establishment would prefer. They are not malicious about it. They are simply themselves, and themselves is a lot.

−2 on Social Opposition Checks in formal, refined, or institutional settings.

### Arrogant
This character believes they are the most capable person in any room they enter. Sometimes they're right. The problem is the times they're not.

−2 on Opposition Checks when deferring to others, accepting help, or following instructions from someone they consider their lesser.

### Suspicious
This character trusts slowly and forgives rarely. It has kept them alive. It has also cost them friends.

−2 on Social Opposition Checks in situations requiring open trust or cooperation. In exchange, +2 on Opposition Checks to detect deception, ambush, or betrayal.

### Cruel
This character has a mean streak — a tendency toward unnecessary harshness when they have the upper hand. They don't have to act on it. But they want to.

When in a position of power over another person, the character must succeed on a Willpower Opposition Check against difficulty 15 to refrain from unnecessary cruelty. −2 on Social Opposition Checks when mercy or restraint is the tactically optimal choice.

### Naive
This character sees the best in people. It is genuinely one of their most endearing qualities. It is also how they keep ending up in the same situation.

−2 on Opposition Checks to detect deception, lies, or manipulation. −2 on Opposition Checks to resist social manipulation or false appeals to trust.

### Jealous
This character keeps score. They're not proud of it. But when someone else gets the recognition, the reward, or the moment they feel they earned, it lands somewhere uncomfortable and stays there.

At creation, name the target of this character's jealousy — a specific person in the character's life. −2 on all Social Opposition Checks involving that target. When the target receives explicit recognition, the jealous character must succeed on a Willpower Opposition Check against difficulty 15. On failure: −2 on all Social Opposition Checks for the remainder of the scene.

The target of jealousy may shift through play as circumstances change — a Narrator and player decision made at a natural narrative turning point.

### People-Pleaser
This character will do almost anything to avoid being the reason someone is unhappy with them. It makes them easy to take advantage of and hard to negotiate against.

−2 on Negotiation Opposition Checks — they concede too much, too early. Must succeed on a Willpower Opposition Check against difficulty 15 to hold a position when the other party expresses disappointment, displeasure, or hurt.

## Phobias

Phobias are triggered responses, not permanent penalties. Each entry specifies its trigger. When the trigger condition arises, the character must immediately make a Willpower Opposition Check against difficulty 15. On success, no penalty applies — but the check must be repeated each time the character re-enters the condition fresh.

### Claustrophobia
Enclosed natural spaces — caves, dense forest, anywhere the sky disappears — produce a disorientation in this character that has nothing to do with cowardice. They simply need to see the horizon.

*Trigger: entering a cave, dense forest, tunnel, or any space without open sky. Willpower Opposition Check, difficulty 15. On failure: −2 on all Opposition Checks for the duration of the exposure.*

### Nyctophobia
This character knows, rationally, that darkness is just the absence of light. Knowing it doesn't help.

*Trigger: complete or near-complete darkness beyond the reach of any light source. Willpower Opposition Check, difficulty 15. On failure: −2 on all Opposition Checks for the duration of the exposure.*

### Pyrophobia
Fire is not a tool to this character. It is a thing that ends things. Encountering it in any significant quantity produces a fear response they cannot fully suppress.

*Trigger: significant fire — beyond a torch or campfire. A burning building, a wall of flame, a fire-breathing creature. Willpower Opposition Check, difficulty 15. On failure: −2 on all Opposition Checks for the duration of the exposure.*

### Acrophobia
Heights are fine. This character is perfectly comfortable with heights — as long as they have something solid underfoot and something solid nearby to fall onto. It's the other kind of height that's the problem.

*Trigger: significant elevation with no solid ground immediately adjacent — a narrow ledge, a rooftop edge, an open skybridge, the back of a flying mount. Willpower Opposition Check, difficulty 15. On failure: −2 on all Opposition Checks for the duration of the exposure.*

### Arachnophobia
Spiders. It's spiders. The character cannot explain it, cannot rationalize it away, and would very much like to stop talking about it.

*Trigger: encountering a spider or spider-like creature of any size. Willpower Opposition Check, difficulty 15. On failure: −2 on all Opposition Checks for the duration of the exposure.*

### Hemophobia
The sight of significant blood — their own or anyone else's — produces in this character an involuntary physical response. Combat is a complicated proposition.

*Trigger: witnessing significant blood loss — a serious wound, a bleeding combatant, or the aftermath of violence. A minor cut does not trigger this. Willpower Opposition Check, difficulty 15. On failure: −2 on all Opposition Checks for the duration of the exposure.*

### Thalassophobia
It is not the water. It is what is in the water — or rather, what might be, in all that dark space beneath the surface where nothing can be seen and nothing can be known. Open sea. Deep lakes. Anything where the bottom is not visible.

*Trigger: being on, in, or immediately adjacent to a large body of deep water — open sea, a deep lake, a river with no visible bottom. Willpower Opposition Check, difficulty 15. On failure: −2 on all Opposition Checks for the duration of the exposure.*

## Compulsions

Compulsions are not choices. When the triggering condition arises, the character must make a Willpower Opposition Check against the listed difficulty. On failure, they are compelled to act on the impulse — they cannot simply decide not to.

### Meddlesome
This character cannot leave well enough alone. Something sealed, forbidden, dangerous, or ancient sits within reach, and the only thing louder than the warning in their head is the question it generates.

*Willpower Opposition Check, difficulty 15. On failure, the character must interact with the object, mechanism, or forbidden thing directly — regardless of consequences.*

### Greedy
Money, treasure, and material advantage occupy a larger portion of this character's attention than they would care to admit.

*Willpower Opposition Check, difficulty 15, when significant material gain is within reach. On failure, the character prioritizes acquiring it above other considerations. Additionally, −2 on Negotiation Opposition Checks in any transaction where the character stands to profit — they push too hard.*

### Addiction
This character has a dependency — on a substance, a behavior, or an experience. The Narrator and player determine the specifics at creation.

When deprived of the addiction for longer than a day, the character suffers −2 on all Opposition Checks. When an opportunity to indulge arises, the character must succeed on a Willpower Opposition Check against difficulty 15 or prioritize it above whatever else they were doing.

### Obsessive
This character fastens onto things. A goal, an enemy, a mystery, an injustice. They are aware they've fastened onto it. They cannot make themselves let go.

The Narrator and player define the obsession at creation. While actively pursuing it, −2 on all Opposition Checks that don't directly advance that goal. Must succeed on a Willpower Opposition Check against difficulty 15 to abandon a line of pursuit — even when continuing has become obviously dangerous.

## Emotional

### Wrathful
This character carries a slow-burning anger that does not require much to ignite. An insult, a betrayal, a perceived slight — any of these can do it.

*Willpower Opposition Check, difficulty 15, when insulted, publicly humiliated, or betrayed. On failure, the character responds with immediate violence or a verbal confrontation that cannot be walked back. −2 on all Opposition Checks while enraged.*

### Prideful
This character cannot admit they don't know something, can't do something, or were wrong about something. It is a profound and specific kind of stubbornness.

−2 on Opposition Checks to ask for help, acknowledge a superior opponent, or accept defeat. −2 on Research Opposition Checks when the answer requires admitting prior ignorance.

### Paranoid
This character assumes the worst about people they don't know — and some they do. Motives are always suspect. Generosity always has a price. Kindness is a vector.

−2 on Social Opposition Checks in situations involving trust or cooperation with anyone outside a small, established circle. +2 on Opposition Checks to detect hidden threats, ambushes, or concealed motives.

### Anxious
This character's mind runs faster than the situation warrants — cataloguing what could go wrong, modeling failure, preparing for outcomes that haven't happened yet. When something unexpected disrupts the plan, the cataloguing gets louder.

−2 on Opposition Checks when acting without preparation or when a plan is disrupted mid-execution. Must succeed on a Willpower Opposition Check against difficulty 12 to act decisively in a situation that has no established plan.

### Self-Doubting
At the moment that matters most, this character hears a voice that asks: *are you sure?* They are rarely sure.

Once per scene, at a moment of the Narrator's choosing, the character must succeed on a Willpower Opposition Check against difficulty 15 or forfeit their action to hesitation. −2 on Opposition Checks to act first or seize initiative in a high-stakes situation.

### Idealistic
This character has principles. They have examined them, they believe in them, and they will not set them aside because the situation would be easier if they did. This is admirable. It is also, occasionally, a disaster.

When the practical choice directly violates the character's principles, they must succeed on a Willpower Opposition Check against difficulty 15 to take it. If they compromise their principles anyway, −2 on all Willpower Opposition Checks for the remainder of the scene.

### Fatalistic
This character believes, at some foundational level, that things tend to go badly. The evidence, in their experience, supports this. They have made their peace with it — which means they sometimes stop trying to prevent it.

−2 on Willpower Opposition Checks against fear or despair. When a situation turns significantly against the group, must succeed on a Willpower Opposition Check against difficulty 12 to keep working the problem rather than cutting losses or taking a reckless, nothing-left-to-lose gamble.

### Haunted
Something happened. The character carries it everywhere. Most of the time they manage. Then something reminds them, and for a moment they are back in it completely.

The Narrator and player define the trauma and its triggers at creation. When confronted with a meaningful reminder of it, the character must succeed on a Willpower Opposition Check against difficulty 15 or suffer −2 on all Opposition Checks for the remainder of the scene. −2 on Willpower Opposition Checks against mind-affecting abilities when the triggering subject is relevant.
