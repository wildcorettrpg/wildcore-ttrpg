# Vampire Lineage

**Type:** Lineage (acquired through play)  
**Prerequisite:** Narratively acquired — see [Lineages](Lineages.md#acquiring-a-lineage)

## Description

Vampirism is an undead transformation — the body remade as a predator that no longer ages, heals, or tires by natural means. A vampire character gains supernatural strength, speed, and resilience, but pays for it with an unending hunger that cannot be fully suppressed. The longer a vampire goes without feeding, the more the hunger asserts itself.

Vampires do not breathe, do not sleep, and do not eat conventional food. They heal almost nothing from rest; blood is their only restorative. Sunlight does not destroy them outright but suppresses their abilities and causes escalating pain over prolonged exposure. Silver suppresses their ability to heal — Blood Healing cannot restore Health from a silver wound while any silver remains in the wound. The silver must be physically removed before feeding will close the injury.

The skill tree represents growing mastery over the vampiric state — learning to control the hunger, develop the supernatural capabilities, and eventually operate at full strength under conditions that cripple lesser-developed vampires.

## Hunger

A vampire must feed (consume blood from a living creature) at least once per session or suffer escalating penalties. The Narrator tracks hunger privately and introduces it as narrative pressure. Hunger does not have a mechanical subsystem — it is a story tool. Vampires who feed regularly experience no mechanical penalty. Those who resist or are denied feeding over multiple sessions will find their capabilities degraded at the Narrator's discretion.

## Training Tier

### Vampire I

**Cost:** 1 SP  
**Prerequisite:** Vampiric transformation (narrative)  
**Effect:** The character completes their transformation. They no longer age, require food or water, or need to breathe. They gain [Bleeding Out](../../Conditions%20and%20Hazards/Conditions.md#bleeding-out) immunity — wounds that would cause [Bleeding Out](../../Conditions%20and%20Hazards/Conditions.md#bleeding-out) in a mortal do not do so automatically; the Narrator may still apply it from sufficiently severe damage. They must feed to recover Health; rest alone restores nothing.

### Vampire II

**Cost:** 1 SP  
**Prerequisite:** Vampire I  
**Effect:** +2 Strength. The vampiric body is physically stronger than it was in life.

### Vampire III

**Cost:** 1 SP  
**Prerequisite:** Vampire II  
**Effect:** **Blood Healing.** The character may feed from a willing or [Incapacitated](../../Conditions%20and%20Hazards/Conditions.md#incapacitated) creature. Initiating the feed costs 1 AP; the vampire must be within reach of the target. Each round the feed continues costs 1 AP. At the end of each round of feeding, **8 Health transfers from the target to the vampire** — the target loses 8 Health and the vampire gains 8 Health. The feed ends when the vampire chooses to stop, the target is no longer willing or [Incapacitated](../../Conditions%20and%20Hazards/Conditions.md#incapacitated), or either party reaches 0 Health.

### Vampire IV

**Cost:** 1 SP  
**Prerequisite:** Vampire III  
**Effect:** +2 Reflex. Vampiric reflexes sharpen beyond mortal limits.

### Vampire V

**Cost:** 1 SP  
**Prerequisite:** Vampire IV  
**Effect:** **Mist Step.** Once per encounter, as a Reaction (1 AP), the character partially dissolves into mist and reappears up to 10 ft. in any direction, avoiding one physical attack that would have connected. This is not a teleport — the character must have unobstructed space at the destination.

## Mastery Tier

### Vampire VI

**Cost:** 1 SP  
**Prerequisite:** Vampire V  
**Effect:** +2 Strength. At this tier, the vampire's physical capabilities are clearly superhuman — they can bend iron, shatter stone, and move faster than the eye comfortably follows.

### Vampire VII

**Cost:** 1 SP  
**Prerequisite:** Vampire VI  
**Effect:** **Dominating Gaze.** Once per scene, the character may attempt to dominate a humanoid target within 30 ft. who can see the vampire's eyes (1 AP). This is a Social Opposition Check (Social vs. the target's Willpower). On success, the target is [Incapacitated](../../Conditions%20and%20Hazards/Conditions.md#incapacitated) for 1 round and must use their next action to follow a single simple command (move here, drop that, stay still). The effect ends immediately if the target takes damage.

### Vampire VIII

**Cost:** 1 SP  
**Prerequisite:** Vampire VII  
**Effect:** **Blood Surge.** Once per encounter, the character may spend 5 Health at the start of their turn (free action) to gain +1 AP this turn and +1d10 to all attack Opposition Checks made this turn. AP granted this way cannot exceed the 5 AP per turn maximum. The Health cost cannot be mitigated or reduced.

### Vampire IX

**Cost:** 1 SP  
**Prerequisite:** Vampire VIII  
**Effect:** **Mist Form.** Once per session, the character may spend 2 AP to fully dissolve into mist for up to 1 minute (10 rounds). In mist form, the character cannot attack, cast spells, or interact with objects, but cannot be physically harmed. They may move at their normal base movement rate in any direction, including through small gaps. They may end the form early at any time (free action).

### Vampire X

**Cost:** 1 SP  
**Prerequisite:** Vampire IX  
**Effect:** **Apex Predator.** The character's Blood Healing (Vampire III) now transfers **16 Health per round** (increased from 8) and may target **any creature regardless of condition** — willing, resistant, or actively fighting. Dominating Gaze (Vampire VII) is no longer limited to once per scene. Sunlight no longer causes pain, though it continues to suppress their vampiric abilities (Narrator's discretion on which, if any, at this tier).

## Notes

1. **Sunlight suppression** is not a mechanical condition with listed penalties — it is Narrator judgment, applied narratively. A newly turned vampire (tier I–IV) in direct sunlight experiences significant suppression; a Vampire X character may find it merely inconvenient. The Narrator adjusts based on tier and story context.
2. **Silver weapons** deal 1 additional damage die against vampires. Silver also suppresses Blood Healing: the vampire cannot use Blood Healing to recover Health from a silver wound while any silver remains in the wound. Removing silver from a wound is a medical action (Narrator-assigned difficulty based on depth and severity).
3. **Feeding on unwilling targets** has social and narrative consequences the rules do not define — these belong to the story, not the stat block. The Narrator and player determine together what lines exist and what it costs to cross them.
